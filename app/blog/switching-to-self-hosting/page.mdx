<BlogHeader title="Switching to Self-Hosting" />

In the past I have relied on [Vercel](https://vercel.com/home) for hosting all of my websites.
Which had been great for my use case at the time,
it was simple to setup without having to maintain any infrastructure of my own.
But recently I have started to self-host a lot of different programs on my own homelab.
Which begged the question, "why am I still relying on a 3rd party for my website?"

Mind you I have been self-hosting many different services such as, [Plex](https://www.plex.tv/personal-media-server/), [Immich](https://immich.app/), and [Kavita](https://www.kavitareader.com/).
All as part of my battle against eliminating pesky subscription services from my life.
As I got better at managing on my docker images, it sort of became an addiction for me to self-host more things.
So of course, my own website was next on the chopping block.
I found that [Coolify](https://coolify.io/) satisfied most of my needs, though it technically wasn't supported on my Linux distro.

I ran into some issues regarding port allocation, which was solved quickly, but the issue seemingly pops up more and more with each container I add.
Aside from that, setting up [Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/) was definitely the most difficult part.
Previously I had used a different domain registrar which had DNS records which were tied into Vercel.
So I decided to switch registrars at the same time as updating DNS records.
But now it should be easier to manage my domains in the future as they are now all centrally located.

Creating tunnels the website was also different from previous setups I had done.
As instead of port mapping I would just expose an internal port on Coolify's internal network.
Then direct the tunnel towards the Coolify proxy itself, then Coolify handles the routing directly to the website.
This in theory should make adding new websites significantly easier as I'd just have to plug in the new domain.
